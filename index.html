About

operational environments

I do not manage what exists. I build what should.

I entered the professional world in 2021 with a simple conviction: understand how things actually work before suggesting how they should change. Starting from an internship, I chose to learn from the ground up. Not just the theory of operations, but what really happens between a decision and its execution. That instinct for observation, for tracing problems to their root, and for building systems that outlast their creator has defined every role I have held since.

Over five years across fashion e-commerce, digital content, and business operations, I have grown from supporting daily functions to leading cross-divisional teams, designing full SOP frameworks, deploying performance monitoring systems, and integrating AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini) as permanent features of how a team works, not temporary experiments. I learn fast, adopt new tools with purpose, and raise the standard of every environment I enter.

I am a person who takes structure seriously but never loses sight of the people inside it. I build accountability frameworks that teams actually follow because they understand the logic. I am direct when clarity is needed, consistent in how I show up, and always working to be better at what I do. Not because someone asks me to, but because that is how I have always approached my career.

▶ Redesigned and standardized operational SOPs for a 70-person team +

When I joined PT Ellyarsyabadi Group, the operational structure had not kept pace with the business's growth. There were no standardized procedures for receiving, sorting, packing, shipping, or customer complaint handling. I assessed the existing workflows first: spending time on the floor with each team to understand how work actually happened, before redesigning the operational framework. The SOPs were co-developed with the teams that would use them, which meant they were followed from day one rather than bypassed. The framework covered the full fulfillment cycle and gave supervisors a clear reference for quality expectations, escalation paths, and performance accountability. This structure enabled the company to scale daily throughput without proportionally increasing errors or coordination overhead.

▶ Led cross-divisional team of 70+ employees including partial HR responsibilities +

I managed the full performance cycle for the operations team, including recruitment for operational roles, structured onboarding for new hires, and performance evaluations tied to divisional KPIs. I handled partial HR responsibilities as an integrated part of operations leadership, not as a separate function. I worked in the field daily, walking the floor, observing processes, identifying bottlenecks in real time, and making quick decisions when exceptions arose rather than waiting for formal reviews. This visibility also allowed me to catch quality and compliance issues before they reached the customer, and to identify team members who were ready for increased responsibility.

▶ Deployed a Google Sheets-based daily performance monitoring system across all divisions +

Before I implemented the monitoring system, performance visibility at PT Ellyarsyabadi was fragmented. Supervisors had no shared view of daily output, attendance, or KPI progress, which made it difficult to identify underperforming areas early. I designed and deployed a Google Sheets-based dashboard accessible to all supervisors that tracked productivity, attendance, and KPIs per division on a daily basis. The system was practical enough that supervisors could update it as part of their normal daily routine without it becoming its own administrative burden. This visibility layer changed how the operations team made decisions: instead of reacting to problems after they had already affected output, we could identify risk earlier and redirect resources before the impact hit.

Discipline is how I show up
At PT Ellyarsyabadi I was on the floor every single working day, not just when things went wrong. Discipline for me is not about being rigid; it is about being consistent. I follow through on commitments, I meet deadlines, and I hold the same standard on a slow day as on a critical one. This is what I bring into every team I lead.
02
I bring professionalism into every environment
One of my goals wherever I work is to raise the professional standard of the place. That means structured communication, clear documentation, defined processes, and accountability that runs in both directions. A company that runs on unclear roles and informal habits is a company with unnecessary friction. I come in to reduce that friction, and I stay until the standard holds on its own.
03
I am clear and direct when it matters
I do not soften feedback that needs to land clearly. At Ellyarsyabadi I gave supervisors real visibility into team output through a daily monitoring dashboard, even when the numbers were uncomfortable. Making operational reality visible to the people responsible for it is what enables correction. Ambiguity in leadership is not kindness; it is a delay to the same problem arriving later and larger.
04
I work systematically and automate what I can
I do not solve problems randomly. I map the process, identify where it breaks, redesign the structure, and then look for what can be automated so the system holds without constant manual effort. I use Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini as genuine parts of my daily workflow for structured thinking, drafting, analysis, and decision support. Technology is a tool. I use it the way it was meant to be used.
